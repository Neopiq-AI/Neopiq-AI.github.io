Pricing

One tier for each level of the product. Free is the Level 0 tier, Trial is the Level 1 tier, Enterprise is the Level 2 tier. Each tier fully unlocks the one level it is named for and includes everything in the tier below it. Only the Free tier is open — and the reason is not the obvious one.

Why only Free is open, when Level 2 is already running

Until recently this page said only the Free tier was open because only Level 0 was built. That is no longer true and we are not going to leave it standing. Level 2 is built. ChatAdmin executes inside a real Azure subscription today, behind an Approve / Reject gate on every change.

What does not exist is any way to hand it to you. ChatAdmin is designed to run inside your subscription; so far it has only ever run inside ours, and there is no sign-up, no login on the chat and no separation between tenants to change that. Built is an engineering fact. Open is a commercial one. On this page the two disagree, and showing the disagreement is more useful to you than tidying it away.

The practical consequence is unchanged: the only thing anyone can be given access to today is the free local check, and it costs nothing.

Why the local check is free, and stays free

It runs on your hardware, so it costs us nothing to serve. Metering it would only slow down the one thing that makes this business work: you finding out that the findings are real before anybody has to ask permission for anything. Every paid tier includes it unlimited, because every tier includes everything in the tier below.

Why the expensive part runs on your Azure bill

Level 2 work is intended to run inside your own subscription. That means the compute is billed to you by Microsoft on the agreement you already have, at the price you already negotiated — and it means your data never has to move to us for the heavy work to happen. We orchestrate; you own the environment.

That is the model on both pillars, and for ChatAdmin it is the whole point: it is designed to execute inside your subscription, on your identity and your Microsoft bill, rather than reaching into your estate from a service of ours.

For D365 it is still a design with no deployment. For ChatAdmin the mechanism already runs — it executes inside a subscription and acts on it — but that subscription is ours, so today none of this is billed to anybody. What it would cost, and who pays for what, is exactly the decision that has not been taken.

What you are not paying for

No implementation project, no per-report development cycle, and no seat licences for people who only want to read a finding. If any of that changes as the commercial model is decided, this page changes with it.
