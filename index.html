From understanding to action, not to a recommendation

ChatAdmin is not an adviser that tells you which portal blade to open. It executes the change you approved and tells you what it read back afterwards. Every request runs through the same eight steps; the green one is yours.

  1. 1 Understand your intent, in your words
  2. 2 Inspect the real state of the environment
  3. 3 Reason across the Microsoft areas involved
  4. 4 Propose the change on a card
  5. 5 Approve you click Approve or Reject
  6. 6 Execute exactly the steps on the card
  7. 7 Verify by reading the result back
  8. 8 Explain what happened, and what did not

Human control, tenant safety, trust

The objection to a tool with administrative access is never capability. It is control: what does it do when nobody is watching, which environment is it about to change, and can I prove it afterwards.

Every change waits for a person

Reads run without interruption. Every change stops at an Approve / Reject card showing the exact steps and parameters. An unanswered card declines itself after five minutes. Nothing is ever approved by software — not by a confidence score, not by a trusted list.

What a card contains

The environment is named on the card, and the card is bound to it

Every card names the tenant or subscription it is about and the identity ChatAdmin acts with, and is bound to that environment when it is raised: at approval time the environment is read again, and a change of context refuses the approval, so an approved plan can never run somewhere else. Who approved is recorded with the signed-in person.

Which tenant is affected

Verified, then audited

After execution ChatAdmin re-reads the object and reports that state, not its intent. A plan that stops at an error says which steps ran and which did not. Every message, card and decision lands in a session transcript with secrets removed.
