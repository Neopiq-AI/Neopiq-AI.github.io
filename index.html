Pillar 1 · built and running today

ChatAdmin — Azure administration, by conversation

You say what you want, instead of working out which blade it lives in. Ask in plain English for a resource group, a storage account, a virtual network, a key vault, a Log Analytics workspace or a Container App, and it works out the calls and chains the steps. It looks things up without interrupting you, and before it changes anything it stops and waits for Approve or Reject. Unanswered for five minutes, it declines. Every action is audited. It signs in to Azure by managed identity, so there are no credentials sitting in the container.

This is the pillar we lead with, and the one we expect to reach the wider audience: administering Azure is a far more common job than running D365 master planning. That is our expectation, not a measurement — we have no customers on either pillar yet.

The model is that ChatAdmin runs inside your own Azure subscription — your tenant, your identity, your audit trail, nothing about your estate exported to us. That is what makes it Level 2 in our ladder: administering a cloud means acting inside it.

What you cannot do yet: sign up. Today the same agent runs against our subscription — a demonstration of the mechanism, not a deployment of the model. There is no login on the chat and no tenant separation, so it cannot be pointed at yours. The full list of limits.

The same principle runs through both pillars

A machine may read whatever it likes. A person decides what changes. That is not a policy statement bolted on afterwards — on both pillars it is the thing that was built first.

The agent stops and asks

Reading your Azure estate runs straight through. Every change raises an Approve / Reject card and the agent waits. Silence is not consent: an unanswered card declines itself after five minutes, so nothing happens while nobody is watching.

How the approval gate works

Pillar 2 · nothing leaves your machine

The security conversation about the free check is a short one

It is a web page that reads your CSV files locally. No upload, no account, no login, no cookie, no analytics and no server to send anything to. Nothing is stored either — closing the tab is the delete button.

You do not have to take that on trust. Open your browser's network tab while it runs, or pull the network cable first.

No procurement step. No security review. No integration project. One person and one export.

Pillar 1 · no credentials in the container

The security conversation about the agent is a longer one, and we start it

It authenticates by managed identity, so no certificate or client secret is baked into the image. Secrets are masked in approval cards and redacted from results and logs. Limits are enforced in the dispatcher, outside the model: out-of-scope actions are refused before any Azure call happens.

The uncomfortable parts, stated by us rather than found by you: in the running demonstration the identity holds Owner on the subscription, which is broader than the least-privilege model it should ship with; the audit trail cannot yet name the person who approved a change, because there is no sign-in; and no customer subscription has ever been connected to it.
