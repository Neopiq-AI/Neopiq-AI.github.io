Solutions · MSPs and IT service providers · in development

Operate across customers quickly without losing control of which environment you are changing

The MSP proposition is not “the business version for more customers”. We are designing ChatAdmin for professional multi-customer operation of Microsoft Azure, Microsoft Entra, Microsoft 365 and Microsoft Power Platform: one technician identity, many environments, the active customer always visible, every approval bound to the environment it was raised in. This page says which of that exists today. Nothing on it is claimed as available unless labelled so.

The design, and what exists of it

One engine, not a separate product. The ChatAdmin that administers one tenant is the one that will administer tens or hundreds; what scales is tenant discovery, switching, access control and history — not the reasoning.

One identity, many environments in development

A technician signs in once with a Microsoft work identity (the sign-in is live) and operates the environments they are authorised for, with ChatAdmin acting under the technician’s own Microsoft rights in each. Access is scoped per managed tenant: the same technician can be Standard in customer A, Observe in customer B, Custom in C and have no access to D. The organisation model and per-tenant access are being built now; today the product is connected to one environment by its operator.

Tenant-bound execution available now

Every card is bound to the environment it was raised in — tenant id, scope, identity — and that environment is re-read at approval time. A changed context refuses the approval; an action prepared for customer A can never become executable against customer B because the technician switched the view. Binding each approval to the exact transaction on the card (operation, targets, parameters) is being added. in development

Explicit active context available now, one environment

Every card and the chat header name the environment and the identity ChatAdmin acts with. With one connected environment that is simple; the same line becomes the active-customer indicator when there are many.

Technician control, approvals, audit available now

Reads run at once; every change is a card answered by a signed-in person — with that area’s key in the Neopiq-operated environment, with the technician’s own sign-in and no key in a customer organisation (in development); five minutes of silence declines it; every message, card and decision is in a session transcript; Microsoft’s own logs show the same changes under the identity ChatAdmin acted with.

Customer search, recent tenants, switching planned

Enough native control plane to operate safely: customer and tenant search, the active-customer indicator, recent and favourite tenants, rapid switching, connected environments with their permission state, per-customer history and approvals.

What we will not build

Not a PSA, not an RMM, not a billing system, not a general ITSM. Those are your existing tools and our integration surfaces. ChatAdmin’s job is Microsoft operational intelligence and governed execution.

Tenant context as part of the security boundary

Cross-tenant execution is the critical safety concern of multi-customer operation. The design makes the environment identity part of every executable plan, not a label on the screen. Conceptually, the technician sees this at all times and on every card:

concept The multi-customer strip above is a design illustration. What exists today is the single-environment line on every card and in the header, and the binding of every card to its environment.

Where customer names, domains or identities are ambiguous, ambiguity is resolved before execution, never after. Switching is optimised for speed without weakening certainty: a switch changes what you look at, never what an already-raised card will do. Tenant isolation comes from the signed-in tenant context checked on the server, never from a customer-specific web address.

The same product, growing with the customer planned

There is no SME edition, enterprise edition or MSP edition, and nobody is asked which they are. One organisation, tenant, user and policy model sits underneath, and the interface shows more only when it becomes useful: a single person with one tenant sees a minimal ChatAdmin and no selector; a second person brings Users & Access and Activity pages for the organisation’s ChatAdmin administrator; several managed tenants bring a visible tenant switcher, a tenant inventory and cross-tenant reporting, with the target tenant unambiguous on every card and no approval surviving a switch; a second Neopiq product brings an app switcher. An MSP’s daily work stays chat-first; the pages are for access review, inventory, reporting and audit. The organisation model that makes this possible is in development; the switcher and the cross-tenant pages are planned on top of it.

Working from your existing environment

Technicians should not have to abandon their operating environment to use ChatAdmin. The long-term operating concept: your platform provides the customer and request context, ChatAdmin provides Microsoft-domain reasoning, controlled execution and verification, and the result goes back to the system the request came from. Integration depth grows in levels; every level below is labelled.
