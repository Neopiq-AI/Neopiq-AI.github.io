We build digital employees, and we are specific about where each one stops

Two of them. ChatAdmin administers Azure through a conversation and stops dead before every change until a person approves it. D365 intelligence reads your Dynamics 365 planning data and stops at the diagnosis, because it never writes anything back at all.

The second one exists because most tools that look at ERP data can tell you a cell is empty, and very few can tell you that a lead time of 30 days has been contradicted by your own receipts for two years, that there is no safety stock behind it, and that this is why the same customer keeps getting a late delivery. The first exists because the objection to an agent with cloud credentials is never capability — it is control.

The problem we set out to fix

ERP systems are configured once, by people who were right at the time. Then the supplier changes, the volumes move, a product is discontinued, a plant is added — and the settings stay exactly as they were. The system keeps obeying them perfectly. The results get steadily worse, and the blame lands on planners, on suppliers, on the ERP itself, on anything except the parameter that quietly stopped being true.

Meanwhile the tool that could find it arrives late, because getting it approved takes longer than running it. A security review, a procurement step, an integration project — and by then the shortage has already shipped, or not shipped, which is worse.

What we do about it

On the D365 side we put the cheapest, safest form of the analysis first. Rules and arithmetic, running on your own machine, over an export you already know how to produce. Nothing moves, so there is nothing to approve, so the first useful answer arrives on day one instead of in quarter three. Only when that has proved useful does anything heavier make sense.

On the admin side that order is impossible — administering a cloud means acting inside it, and there is no read-only version of creating a resource group. So the first thing built there was not a capability but a brake: an Approve / Reject gate that every change has to pass, that nothing can skip, and that declines by itself if nobody answers within five minutes. The capabilities were added behind it afterwards. It is also why ChatAdmin is designed to execute inside your own subscription rather than reaching into it from ours — if a machine is going to act on your cloud, it should do so on your side of the line, under your identity.

What we hold ourselves to

Evidence, or it does not get printed

Every finding names its objects, quotes the numbers it compared, states the business impact and the likely cause, recommends a specific change, and points at an owner. No unexplained recommendations, ever. If we cannot show the working, we do not make the claim.

Your data stays yours

The free check moves nothing at all. The steps we are building are designed so that what crosses the boundary is results, never your records — and so that the heavy work eventually runs inside your own environment, on your own controls, rather than in ours. The admin agent already works that way: it acts inside a subscription rather than pulling anything out of one.

A person decides what changes

Nothing writes back to your ERP, today or in any planned step — the D365 pillar produces a work list and stops there. The admin agent does change things, in Azure, and every one of those changes is shown to a person first and does not happen unless it is approved. Silence counts as no.

We would rather be checkable than impressive

Thresholds are written in plain rule files so you can argue with them. Confidence is reported as judgement rather than dressed up as probability. Checks that could not run are listed instead of quietly skipped. This site claims no customers, no results and no certifications, because we do not have them yet.

How that turns into a working check

Where we actually are

Stated plainly, because you will find out anyway and it is better if you hear it here.

  • What exists and you can use: the local check for Dynamics 365 planning and master data — 26 checks, running free in your browser, over extracts you produce yourself. No account, nothing uploaded.
  • What exists and you cannot use: ChatAdmin. It is deployed and running, and it creates and manages real Azure resources behind an approval gate. It is designed to execute inside your subscription — your tenant, your identity, your audit trail — but so far it has only ever run inside ours, because there is no sign-up, no login on the chat and no separation between tenants. That is a product gap, not a waiting list.
  • What does not exist: Power Platform administration, ChatAdmin's template-deployment, governance and devops actions, any route to install ChatAdmin into a customer's subscription, the cloud explanation and reporting step, and the in-tenant execution step for D365. They are described on this site in the future tense, under visible markers, and nowhere else.
  • What is unfinished about the part that runs: in the demonstration ChatAdmin's identity holds Owner on the subscription, which is broader than the least-privilege model it should ship with; and its audit trail cannot yet name the person who approved a change, because there is nobody to name until sign-in exists.
  • What has not been proved: the D365 thresholds have not been tuned against a real customer tenant, and the sample data that demonstrates the checks is synthetic. We say so on the pages where it matters.
  • What we have not got: customers we can name, published results, certifications, or a price. When those exist they will appear here; until then this site will keep being conspicuously empty where competitors put logos.
