How a sentence becomes a verified change — and why a person sits in the middle of it

ChatAdmin administers Microsoft Azure, Microsoft Entra, Microsoft 365 and Microsoft Power Platform. This page follows one request through the eight steps every request takes, shows what the Approve / Reject card and the result actually contain, sets out the stages of access and the organisation model we are building, and ends with the technology behind one coherent ChatAdmin.

The eight steps

The same loop for a one-line question and for a joiner that touches two Microsoft areas. Confidence is never authorisation: step five is a human, every time.

  1. 1 Understand what you asked for, in your words; one question back if a decision cannot be read from the environment
  2. 2 Inspect the real state — users, groups, seats, teams, resources — with reads that need no approval
  3. 3 Reason about which areas are involved and what the environment already implies (a usage location, the only licence with a free seat)
  4. 4 Propose one card per area, with every step and parameter, after the pre-checks that catch a wasted approval
  5. 5 Approve you read the card and click Approve or Reject — in the Neopiq-operated environment with that area’s key; in your own organisation with your own Microsoft sign-in, no key (in development); silence for five minutes is a no
  6. 6 Execute exactly the steps on the card, in order, stopping at the first error
  7. 7 Verify by reading each object back from Microsoft and showing that state
  8. 8 Explain what was done, what was not, and what needs a person in the portal

What a card contains

Approval objects emphasise the outcome, then show the exact steps. You approve a specific change to a specific environment — never a summary of one.

Pre-checks: sign-in name free · group exists · seat free. A failing pre-check refuses the step before the card; a check that cannot run never blocks a card.

Line by line

  • The area and the action, in words: which Microsoft area is about to change, a single change or a plan of up to twelve steps behind one approval.
  • The environment: the tenant or subscription the card is about and the identity ChatAdmin acts with there. The card is bound to that environment when it is raised, and the environment is read again when you approve: if it has changed, or no longer answers, the approval is refused and nothing is forwarded.
  • The facts line: live or simulated, the five-minute countdown, what answers the card (today: that area’s key).
  • Every step with its parameters. Secrets are masked. A delete restates the exact name it will delete.
  • The preview: what the matching read found — what a role allows, what a delete would remove, the pre-checks that already passed.
  • Approve / Reject, answered by a person and recorded with the signed-in operator who answered. In the Neopiq-operated environment that means typing that area’s approver key into the page. In your own organisation there will be no key: your own Microsoft sign-in is the approval, bound to this one transaction. in development Nothing else can answer a card.

What a result looks like

Never “done” because a request was submitted. After each change the object is read back, and the three outcomes look different on purpose.

executed
3 of 3 steps ran
verified
the account read back: enabled, in the group, licence assigned
audit
card shown, approved, every step recorded
partial
2 of 3 steps ran; step 3 failed: no free seat
verified
the account and the membership exist; the licence does not
next
buy a seat, then ask again — nothing is retried on its own
declined
nothing ran
why
rejected by you, or unanswered for five minutes
audit
the card and the decision are still recorded

A plan is not a transaction: steps that ran before a failure stay applied, and the result says exactly which ones. Where a change cannot be verified by a read, ChatAdmin says “submitted, not re-verified” rather than inventing a state.

The stages of access

Signing in and allowing ChatAdmin to administer your environment are different trust decisions, and they stay separate. Each stage is labelled with what exists today.

  1. Demonstration available now

    A sample environment, clearly marked, not connected to anything of yours. The real interaction pattern: intent, inspection, proposal, your approval, simulated execution, a verified-looking result. See ChatAdmin in Action.

  2. Microsoft sign-in available now

    You sign in to ChatAdmin with your Microsoft work account. ChatAdmin learns your name, your sign-in name and your organisation’s tenant id, and nothing more: signing in gives no access to administer any environment. The sign-in is live on ChatAdmin and is required; today it admits Neopiq’s own organisation, which is where evaluations run.

  3. Connect your organisation in development

    A verified administrator of your Microsoft tenant connects your organisation to ChatAdmin — nothing is installed in your tenant. From then on ChatAdmin acts with each signed-in person’s own Microsoft rights, asks for the specific permissions a capability needs and explains each one before Microsoft’s consent screen (what, why, which feature, reading or changing, whether an administrator must consent), and every change is approved by that person’s own sign-in. There are no Neopiq keys. The model, in full.

  4. Grow without migrating planned

    The same ChatAdmin exposes more only when it becomes useful: a second person brings Users & Access and Activity pages for your administrator (in development); several managed tenants bring a visible tenant switcher with per-tenant access, and a second Neopiq product an app switcher (planned). No separate edition for larger organisations or MSPs.

Your own organisation on ChatAdmin in development

This is the customer model we are building now, in the words of the design we build to. None of it is live yet: today ChatAdmin administers the one environment a Neopiq operator has connected. Each point below is a rule of the design, not a feature you can use today.

Connect, do not install

ChatAdmin is hosted by Neopiq. Nothing is installed in your tenant or in an Azure subscription; you connect and authorise your Microsoft environment to ChatAdmin, and no Azure subscription is required. Microsoft 365 and Microsoft Entra are the base; Microsoft Azure and Microsoft Power Platform are additional surfaces you add when you use them, never prerequisites. You never need to know or install the specialists behind ChatAdmin.

Your sign-in is your approval — no Neopiq keys

In your organisation there is no approver key. You approve each change yourself, signed in with Microsoft, and each approval is bound to one transaction: your tenant, you, the operation, the targets, the material parameters, the risk level, an expiry. If any of that changes, the approval is void and ChatAdmin asks again. “You approved something earlier in this conversation” never carries over to a different change. Software never generates an approval.

Microsoft is the ceiling

The browser proves you to ChatAdmin; ChatAdmin obtains access to Microsoft for that request through Microsoft’s supported delegated mechanisms (validated per Microsoft surface), acting with your own rights — and Microsoft, not ChatAdmin, decides what those rights allow. ChatAdmin settings can expose that authority, make it read-only, narrow it or add confirmation; they can never widen it. A Neopiq subscription, an administrator flag or a custom setting adds no Microsoft rights to anyone.

Observe, Standard, Custom

Each person has one operational mode per organisation. Observe is read only: ChatAdmin reads, analyses, diagnoses, explains, recommends and prepares plans, and executes nothing. Standard is everything your Microsoft identity may already do within the areas your organisation has enabled, with confirmation scaled to risk rather than capability removed. Custom is a restriction below Standard that your organisation sets — users, groups and licences allowed, Exchange read only, privileged roles blocked through ChatAdmin, for example — without touching your Microsoft roles.

Confirmation scaled to risk

Every operation carries a risk class, the same across all four Microsoft areas. Normal (reads and reversible preparations): only the necessary confirmation. Approval required (any change): the exact transaction on a card and your explicit approval. High (deletions, removals of access, role, permission or credential changes): the impact stated and a typed confirmation of the target’s name. Extreme (tenant-wide or security-boundary changes, and every change to ChatAdmin’s own control plane): the strongest safeguards, including a fresh sign-in. Greater risk means more friction, not a smaller product.

The ChatAdmin administrator

Independent of the mode: who may administer your ChatAdmin organisation — its people, their modes, Custom restrictions, settings, other administrators. It adds no Microsoft rights. The first administrator is not whoever signs in first; it is a Microsoft identity that passes the administrative verification of your tenant, and it starts as Standard + ChatAdmin administrator (or chooses Observe or Custom) and simply starts working — no permission matrix to configure first. Access is managed by conversation: “give Bob Standard access” raises a card with Observe / Standard / Custom and the administrator checkbox; Users & Access and Activity pages give the overview on the same data.

When people leave

Disable or delete someone in Microsoft and their ChatAdmin access is suspended automatically; an administrator flag confers nothing while suspended; the history keeps their name on what they approved; nobody is promoted automatically. If no usable administrator remains, an eligible current administrator of your tenant recovers the organisation’s administration themselves — verified by Microsoft, audited, the roster shown afterwards — not by asking Neopiq.

What we never do

No software approves a change; an automated check may only reject one. No standing Neopiq key in your organisation. No customer token at rest: the access ChatAdmin obtains for a request lives for that request and is never stored. Nothing installed in your tenant. Nothing beyond your own Microsoft rights. No separate SME, enterprise or MSP edition — one product that shows more only when it becomes useful.

What this looks like for a small organisation: sign in with Microsoft, connect the organisation, be verified as its administrator, start working. The deeper controls exist underneath and appear when a second person, a second tenant or a second product makes them useful.

The technology behind one ChatAdmin

You talk to one product. Underneath, four Microsoft-area specialists do the work and a coordinator keeps the conversation whole. This is architecture, not something you have to operate.

Four specialists, one per Microsoft area

Azure, Microsoft Entra, Microsoft 365 and Power Platform are each served by a separate agent: its own identity in that area, its own tool set (reads and changes), its own guardrails enforced before any Microsoft call, its own approval card (and, in the Neopiq-operated environment, its own key), its own redacted session transcript. A specialist never reaches outside its area — the Entra agent calls nothing but Microsoft Graph; the Azure agent cannot create credentials.

A coordinator that cannot approve

The layer you talk to plans with the tools the specialists actually publish, asks them in turn inside your request, carries facts between them (the new account’s id from Entra to Azure, a licence hand-off from Microsoft 365 to Entra), and answers as one voice. It holds no key, has no approve tool, runs nothing between your turns, and its relays are bounded in code. Every change is still the specialist’s own card, answered by you.

Where the language model sits

The model understands the request and chooses among the built-in actions; it fills in parameters you then see on the card. It does not decide what is allowed: the ceilings, the pre-checks, the approval gate, the redaction and the audit are code around the model, and a refusal by rule cannot be argued away in a prompt.

Where it runs today

In a Neopiq-operated environment on Microsoft Azure, scaled to zero when idle, connected by a Neopiq operator to one Microsoft tenant. The identities it uses per area, what is stored and where, and how access is removed are on the trust page.
