Privacy policy

This site is static HTML and CSS. It sets no cookies, loads nothing from anywhere else and has no form to submit, and the few scripts it runs are described in the note below. Pages with a sign-in action wake the ChatAdmin service in the background, so that it is ready when you open it, with a request that carries no cookie and no identifier. The only personal data processed when you visit is the server-log record that any web server keeps when it serves a page. This notice covers the website, the free check and correspondence with us, and, in a section of its own, ChatAdmin: the product that “Open ChatAdmin” leads to, which you sign in to with Microsoft.

ChatAdmin

ChatAdmin is Neopiq’s product for administering Microsoft Entra, Microsoft 365, Microsoft Azure and Microsoft Power Platform by conversation. It is a separate service from this website: “Open ChatAdmin” on these pages leads to it. Organisations sign in with Microsoft and use it in their own Microsoft tenant, and managed service providers use it for the tenants of the customers they manage. This section says what ChatAdmin processes when you use it, where it is kept and how long. All of ChatAdmin’s own records are kept in Neopiq’s Microsoft Azure storage in the Switzerland North region, where ChatAdmin also runs.

Signing in with Microsoft

  • You sign in with a Microsoft work account. ChatAdmin asks Microsoft for your identity only, not for access to anything in your tenant, and learns your name, your sign-in name, your tenant id and Microsoft’s internal identifier for your account. Personal Microsoft accounts are refused.
  • The sign-in token Microsoft issues is checked once and then discarded; it is not stored.
  • ChatAdmin sets one cookie, chatadmin_session: a random identifier with nothing inside it, valid for at most eight hours. Behind it ChatAdmin keeps a session record (your name, sign-in name, tenant id, internal identifier and the time you signed in), so that a restart of the service does not sign you out. An expired session record is deleted at the latest a day after its last use. In the browser, ChatAdmin keeps the conversation’s identifier and the Microsoft sign-in library’s tokens in the tab’s session storage, which the browser clears when the tab is closed.
  • Your name, sign-in name and tenant id are shown on every approval card and recorded in the conversation’s transcript, so that every decision says who made it.

Reading your Microsoft tenant

  • ChatAdmin reads a customer’s tenant only under the signed-in person’s own Microsoft sign-in, after an administrator of that tenant has consented to ChatAdmin’s permissions. It never has more rights than the person using it. ChatAdmin's software only reads in customers’ tenants; changes are not open for customers. Some Microsoft permissions it asks for are not limited to reading: for Azure, Power Platform and Dataverse, Microsoft offers only permissions that also allow changes within your own rights, and two Microsoft 365 permissions (Files.Read.All, Sites.Read.All) would allow reading the content of files you can read. ChatAdmin does not use them for changes or for file content; that limit is in ChatAdmin's code. Some areas ask for their own consent the first time you use them.
  • The Microsoft access ChatAdmin obtains for a request is used for that request and is not stored.
  • What is read depends on what you ask: for example users, groups, role holders, licences, sign-in error codes, directory audit entries, Conditional Access policies, consent settings and sensitivity-label definitions; mailbox sizes and permissions, Teams and SharePoint membership, Teams app status and usage reports with per-user activity counts; Azure resources and costs; Power Platform environments and Copilot Studio agents; Intune devices with their names and primary users; and Microsoft Defender security incidents and alerts, which can name affected users, devices and mailboxes. ChatAdmin does not read the body of messages, calendar entries, chats or files; alert, audit and report records can contain message subjects and file names.
  • What the reads return is shown to you in the conversation and recorded in its transcript.

The language model

  • ChatAdmin’s answers are written by Anthropic’s Claude models, called through Anthropic’s API under Neopiq’s own company account. For every turn Anthropic receives the conversation: what you type, ChatAdmin’s instructions, and what the reads of your tenant return.
  • Before read results reach the model, values that look like credentials (passwords, keys, tokens) are removed. Names, sign-in names, e-mail addresses and other directory data are not removed, and what you type is sent as you typed it.
  • Anthropic’s commercial terms apply to this traffic; they state that Anthropic may not train models on customer content from its services. Anthropic deletes the conversation within 30 days; content that its systems flag under its Usage Policy is kept up to 2 years. Neopiq has requested Zero Data Retention; once Anthropic has activated it, Anthropic does not store the conversation after returning its answer, except where needed to comply with law or to combat misuse. Neopiq’s deletion described below does not reach Anthropic.

Conversation transcripts and logs

  • Transcripts. Each conversation is recorded: your messages, ChatAdmin’s answers, the reads it ran and their results (shortened to a bounded length), the approval cards shown and their outcome, and the signed-in identity. Values that look like credentials are removed before anything is written; personal data is not. Neopiq’s staff read a transcript only for a support request about it, to investigate a security incident or a malfunction, or to carry out a deletion or export; nobody at Neopiq browses transcripts. You can download a conversation from your own browser. A daily job deletes transcripts 90 days after the day of the conversation.
  • Operations logs. Microsoft Azure Log Analytics, in Switzerland North, keeps ChatAdmin’s technical logs for 30 days: the requests the service answered, start-up lines, for each transcript entry a line with identifiers and its size but not its text, and the storage account’s access records (which record was read, written or deleted, by which identity, never its content). No password, token or key is logged.

Your organisation, its plan and its activity log

  • Organisation and members. For each customer tenant: the tenant id and name, who set the organisation up, and which ChatAdmin areas it uses. For each person who has signed in: name, sign-in name, internal identifier, their access in ChatAdmin (Observe, Standard or Custom, with any restrictions), whether they are a ChatAdmin administrator, their state, and the dates of changes and of the last sign-in.
  • Activity log. Every change of access, every approval and every consent step is one line: who, what, before and after, when. The organisation’s ChatAdmin administrators see it under Activity.
  • Plan and billing state. The customer record (tenant, the sign-in name of the person who started, its progress), the plan, the trial, the subscription’s status and what the plan allows; for a managed service provider’s trial request, also the company name, the requesting person’s sign-in name as the contact and the number of customers it manages.
  • Waiting list. When today’s first-look places or this week’s free-trial places are taken, you can leave your company name, a contact e-mail address, the plan you are interested in and an optional note, so that we can write to you when a place opens. We keep these details until your trial starts (they then become part of your customer records), until you leave the waiting list (they are deleted at once), or until you ask us to delete them; they are never used for anything else.
  • First look. When an administrator starts the free first look, ChatAdmin records the tenant, the administrator’s sign-in name as the contact, the company name and the start time, and for each question the tenant id and the time, to count the day’s questions.
  • Invitations. Each organisation has one invitation link with an opaque code, which is not derived from its tenant id. For it ChatAdmin keeps the code, the number of other organisations that started their first look through it, and for each of them a one-way hash of its tenant id (never the id itself) with the date. The invited organisation’s record notes, with the date, a hash of the inviting tenant. Neopiq sends no invitation e-mail: you share the link yourself, from Outlook, Teams or LinkedIn or by copying it, and what you send goes through those services, not through Neopiq.
  • Checking that an organisation is an IT service provider. When an organisation asks for the MSP trial, ChatAdmin checks, with the administrator’s own Microsoft sign-in and the permissions already granted, whether Microsoft shows partner contracts for the tenant; or it reads once the public home page of one of the tenant’s verified domains and has the language model (Anthropic, see the language model) analyse it once. ChatAdmin keeps only the result (verified, pending a review, confirmed or declined), how it was reached and the kind of evidence - never the page’s text - and, for a request a person at Neopiq reviews, the contact, the company name and the domains. While a review is pending, the fact that a person of one of those domains connected another tenant is noted on it as evidence.
  • How long. These records are kept while the organisation exists and are not deleted by age. Two exceptions: the daily log of plan and billing events is deleted after 90 days, and a trial request that never became a trial is deleted 90 days after it was last changed. The first-look, invitation and verification records are kept like the trial records. Everything of an organisation that leaves is deleted on request (below); an inviting organisation’s count then keeps only the hash of that organisation.

Managed service providers

  • A managed service provider (MSP) connects each of its customers through a single-use, time-limited link that the customer’s own administrator opens; that administrator consents in the customer’s tenant.
  • The MSP’s technicians work in a customer’s tenant under their own guest account in that tenant, with the rights the customer gives that guest. One conversation works on one customer, and every card names the customer and the tenant. Activity in a customer’s tenant is recorded in the MSP’s activity log and in the customer’s own.
  • The MSP’s records about each customer hold: its name and the MSP’s own reference, the tenant id and name, the state of the connection and of the consent (which permissions, when, by whom, with which roles), the Microsoft services detected, the connection’s health and last error, the technicians (sign-in names, first and last use) and the MSP’s safety settings for that customer. They stay with the MSP’s organisation, also after a customer is disconnected, as its history.
  • The MSP’s overview of its customers reads each tenant separately, with that tenant’s own access, and holds the results in memory only.

What ChatAdmin knows about your tenant

Since 17 November 2026 ChatAdmin keeps a tenant profile: a few facts about a customer’s tenant that it remembers between conversations. Before a profile is first stored, the organisation is told in ChatAdmin, under Organization, where its administrators can see every fact and delete them.

  • What it holds. Which Microsoft services the tenant has (for example Microsoft Entra, Microsoft 365, Azure, Power Platform, Intune); its licences, by Microsoft’s product name, with the numbers purchased, assigned and available; and totals such as the numbers of users, guests, mailboxes, subscriptions, environments and devices. Each fact carries the time it was read. It also keeps a short list of the last changes to those numbers (for example a licence count going from 20 to 25), each with its date. For very small tenants, and tenants whose size is not known yet, it holds only which services are present.
  • What it never holds. Names, sign-in names, e-mail addresses, device or group names, anything about an individual user, and the content of messages or files.
  • Where it comes from. Only from reads that already happen in a conversation or in the MSP’s overview; it adds no reads and no Microsoft permission. A change is never based on it: every change reads the tenant again.
  • What it is used for. Only to serve that customer: better answers, its MSP’s view of it (for an MSP, also totals across that MSP’s own customers — counts only, shown only to the MSP’s own members, a number from one or two customers shown as “fewer than 3”), and recommendations to that customer about its own tenant. Operational recommendations, for example unassigned licences, are shown until the customer’s ChatAdmin administrator switches them off. A recommendation to buy a Microsoft product is shown only after that administrator has switched such recommendations on; the profile is never used to recommend ChatAdmin’s own plans or upgrades. It is never used for Neopiq’s marketing, never shown to other customers, and never combined across customers except in an MSP’s totals for its own customers. Like everything in a conversation, facts from it reach the language model when they are part of a turn.
  • Your control. The customer’s ChatAdmin administrator sees the profile under “What ChatAdmin knows about your tenant” and can delete it there. Creating, changing and deleting it is recorded in the activity log.
  • How long. A profile not refreshed for 90 days is deleted by the daily job. It is also deleted when an MSP disconnects that customer, and with the organisation’s other records.

Deletion, and withdrawing ChatAdmin’s access

  • Deletion on request. Write to contact@neopiq.ai. Neopiq then deletes everything of the tenant: transcripts, organisation, member and activity records, plan and billing records and session records. Neopiq keeps no backup copies of these records, so the deletion is final. It does not reach what Anthropic or Paddle hold under their own terms, nor Microsoft’s own logs in your tenant.
  • Withdrawing access at Microsoft. An administrator of your tenant can remove ChatAdmin’s access at any time in the Microsoft Entra admin center, under Enterprise applications, by removing the applications “ChatAdmin Sign-in” and “ChatAdmin API” or their permissions. A user can review the applications they use at myapps.microsoft.com. Withdrawing the access stops ChatAdmin from reading your tenant; it does not delete Neopiq’s records, which you ask for as above.

Where Neopiq decides about the processing (sign-in, organisation and member records, plans and billing, logs), its legal bases are the contract with your organisation and Neopiq's legitimate interest in providing a working, secure service (GDPR Art. 6(1)(b) and (f); revFADP Art. 31), and for accounting records a legal obligation (Code of Obligations Art. 958f). For what ChatAdmin reads from your tenant and the conversations, Neopiq acts for your organisation, which decides about it.
